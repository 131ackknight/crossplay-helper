Crossplay, Scrabble, and Words with Friends

The three games look alike and score differently. This page is the table the board solver switches when you change the game. Shaded rows are letters whose points are not the same in all three. Open the Scrabble finder or the Words with Friends finder if that is the board in front of you. The Crossplay charts are the tile values and the board layout.

One opening, three totals

This comparison uses one placement in all three games: QUIXOTE across row 8, columns 5 through 11, which the results list calls Across at 8,5. The board is empty, so there is no cross-word, and all seven tiles are new. It is not the highest opening for this rack. It is the same squares, so the gap is the rules. Crossplay's face value is 24 before its two double-letter squares, and the star does not double the word. Scrabble's U is 1 rather than 2, so the face value is 23, and the center star doubles it. Words with Friends matches Crossplay's U at 2, doubles I and O on its own double-letter squares, and does not double the word.

Letter values

Fourteen letters differ in at least one game. A, D, E, F, I, O, Q, R, S, T, X, Z, and the blank score the same in all three. Nine letters differ between Crossplay and Scrabble: B, G, H, J, K, L, U, V, and W. Twelve differ between Words with Friends and Scrabble: B, C, G, H, J, L, M, N, P, U, V, and Y. K and W are the Crossplay changes that Words with Friends does not share. C, M, N, P, and Y are the Words with Friends changes that Crossplay does not share.

A few of those change a decision, not just a table. G is 4 in Crossplay, 2 in Scrabble, and 3 in Words with Friends, so a rack full of G is a different hand in each game. U is 2 outside Scrabble, which is why QUIXOTE's face value drops by a point when you switch to Scrabble. J is 10 in Crossplay and Words with Friends, and 8 in Scrabble. W is 5 only in Crossplay. N is 2 only in Words with Friends, so ordinary tiles pay a little more there while the bingo pays less.

Bonus squares

All three boards are 15 by 15. The counts below are the layouts this solver draws. Scrabble's 17 double-word squares include the center. Crossplay's 8 do not, because the center is a star with no bonus. Crossplay puts a triple letter on each corner and a triple word on the fourth square in from that corner, along the edge. Scrabble puts a triple word on each corner.

Fewer double-word squares means a Crossplay word multiplier is rarer, so the sweep and the 20 triple-letter squares do more of the scoring. Words with Friends, on the board used here, has 38 double-letter squares and a 35-point bingo, so a hook on a double letter can matter more than chasing a bingo that pays less.

How many tiles

Crossplay's bag has 100 tiles. Face value, the points printed on every tile added together, is 210. Scrabble's bag is also 100 tiles and its face value is 187. Crossplay has 40 vowel tiles (A, E, I, O, U). Scrabble has 42. The counts that differ are S, H, and the blank, one more each in Crossplay, and I, N, and U, one fewer each. The full Crossplay bag is the table on the tile values page. This helper does not keep a separate Words with Friends bag. It scores the letters you type with the Words with Friends points above.
